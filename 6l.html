6 Lenses Review and Report

Editorial Diagnostic & Execution Planning System

Input Fields
Prompts
Review the content provided with this prompt through six complementary editorial lenses. The purpose of this review is to: 1. Identify genuine problems or meaningful opportunities for improvement. 2. Diagnose what kind of problem each observation represents. 3. Explain why the issue matters. 4. Recommend the direction of change. 5. Check whether the content creates the understanding it needs to create in the reader. 6. Check whether concepts appear in the right conceptual order and with the necessary dependencies. 7. Check whether the current content faithfully preserves the substantive content and conceptual architecture of the Dumb Doc, when a Dumb Doc is provided. 8. Protect the author's substantive intellectual content. This is a diagnostic review. Do not rewrite the content. Do not create a new version of the content. Do not introduce a new intellectual direction. Do not replace the author's ideas with your own. ================================================== GOVERNING PRINCIPLE Structure is in service of the content, not content in service of the structure. The author's substantive content must be protected. Substantive authorial content may include: propositions arguments explanations interpretations conceptual distinctions principles reasoning examples analogies observations insights conclusions practical techniques philosophical or metaphysical claims Do not remove or dilute substantive content merely for: brevity symmetry neatness flow reader convenience compactness stylistic consistency If important content creates a structural problem, first consider whether the structure, sequence, placement, context, explanation, transition, or delivery should change so that the content works properly. Do not call content unnecessary until its function has been properly considered. ================================================== INTENDED UNDERSTANDING CHECK For every important section, ask: What should the reader understand by the end of this section? Does the content actually create that understanding? Does the reader receive enough context? Is the explanation sufficient? Is the concept introduced at the right time? Is the sequence understandable? Does the reader understand why the idea matters? Can the reader explain the idea in their own words? Can the reader explain why it matters within the reasoning? Can the reader see its relationship with the previous and following ideas? Can the reader recognize or apply the idea in a new but related situation, when application is part of the intended understanding? Can the reader distinguish the idea from a nearby but different idea? Look for understanding gaps caused by: missing context insufficient explanation premature introduction of a concept late introduction of a necessary concept wrong sequence weak transition unclear distinction inadequate example over-compressed explanation unnecessary conceptual jump unclear expression missing implication missing connection between ideas Use this rule: Explain until the necessary understanding is achieved, not until everything the author knows has been said. Do not recommend expansion merely because more explanation is possible. Do not recommend shortening merely because an explanation is long. Judge explanation length by the understanding the reader needs to achieve. ================================================== CONCEPTUAL DEPENDENCY CHECK For important concepts, ask: What does the reader need to know before understanding this? What concept does this idea depend upon? Has the prerequisite already been established? Is the dependent concept being introduced too early? Is it being introduced too late? Does the reader have enough context to understand why the concept appears here? Does the sequence follow the logic of the content? Does the reader have a natural reason to move from one concept to the next? If the content is sound but the position is wrong, diagnose it as a structural or content–structure problem rather than a content problem. ================================================== LENS 1 — SEASONED WRITING EDITOR Examine the writing for: vague language generic statements clichés unnecessary repetition wordiness imprecision awkward expression weak sentences unclear references unnecessarily formal language artificial language mechanical phrasing poor rhythm sentences that obscure rather than clarify meaning Do not recommend substantive deletion merely to make the writing shorter. Distinguish between: language that is difficult because the idea itself is difficult language that is unnecessarily difficult because of poor expression ================================================== LENS 2 — FIRST-TIME READER Read as a reader who has never seen the content before. Look for: confusion re-reading points missing context unfamiliar concepts insufficient explanation unexpected conceptual jumps unclear references writerly assumptions language that feels unnatural to the reader places where interest weakens places where the reader may ask: “Why am I being told this now?” “Why does this matter?” “What does this mean?” “How did we get here?” Compare intended understanding with actual reader understanding. When there is a gap, identify the likely cause. ================================================== LENS 3 — KNOWLEDGEABLE READER Read as an informed and intellectually attentive reader. Look for: overly basic explanation obvious statements predictable reasoning insufficient intellectual value weak conceptual distinction unsupported conceptual leap places where greater precision is necessary places where an important distinction has been blurred places where the reasoning appears thinner than the surrounding argument Do not remove foundational explanation simply because a knowledgeable reader already knows it. Ask whether the foundation is necessary for the intended reader and for the reasoning that follows. ================================================== LENS 4 — “SO WHAT?” LENS Examine the reader-value of the content. Ask: What does this information enable the reader to understand? What insight does it create? What changes in the reader's understanding? Why does this matter within the content? What is the implication? What is the practical or intellectual significance? Does the section merely provide information, or does it create understanding? Does a claim have an implication that has not been made sufficiently clear? Does an explanation lead naturally to a meaningful takeaway? Do not remove content merely because its value is not immediately obvious. First determine whether it serves as: foundation prerequisite argument support conceptual clarification distinction transition preparation for a later idea ================================================== LENS 5 — READER JOURNEY Examine the movement of the reader through the content. Look for: weak opening unclear orientation detours circular movement unnecessary repetition abrupt transitions premature concepts insufficient preparation important ideas appearing too late weak development sudden conclusions disconnected endings missing bridges between ideas lack of a natural reason for the next idea Pay particular attention to: experience → question question → question question → need need → concept concept → explanation explanation → implication concept → concept section → section ending → next beginning Always distinguish between: content that is genuinely unnecessary content that is necessary but currently in the wrong place content that is necessary but insufficiently prepared content that is necessary but insufficiently explained ================================================== LENS 6 — CONTENT FIDELITY & CONCEPTUAL COMPLETENESS Use this lens when a Dumb Doc or other clearly identified source-content document is provided. The purpose of this lens is to compare the current content against the Dumb Doc and determine whether the author's substantive intellectual content and conceptual architecture have been faithfully preserved. Do not treat sentence-by-sentence similarity as the goal. The goal is preservation of meaning, function, reasoning, conceptual relationships, mechanisms, distinctions, and intended intellectual direction. The Dumb Doc is the source of the author's substantive content for this comparison. Check the following: A. SUBSTANTIVE CONTENT FIDELITY Identify the important substantive ideas in the Dumb Doc. For each important idea, determine whether it is: Preserved Compressed Implicit Weakened Altered Missing Distorted Ask: Has any important proposition been omitted? Has any important explanation been removed? Has an important interpretation disappeared? Has an important example or analogy been removed when it performs a necessary conceptual function? Has an important conclusion been weakened? Has substantive content been removed merely to improve flow, brevity, symmetry, or neatness? B. CONCEPTUAL FIDELITY For every major concept in the Dumb Doc, ask: What does this concept mean in the Dumb Doc? What function does it perform? What does it mean in the current content? Is the meaning preserved? Has its scope changed? Has an important dimension disappeared? Has a specific concept been replaced by a more generic term? Has a concept been simplified so much that its original intellectual function is lost? C. CONCEPTUAL DISTINCTION Identify important distinctions made in the Dumb Doc. Ask: Are the distinctions still present? Have two separate concepts been merged? Has one concept been substituted for another? Has terminology caused two distinct concepts to appear identical? Has a distinction that is necessary for the argument disappeared? Pay particular attention when the Dumb Doc distinguishes between concepts that may appear similar on the surface. Do not assume two concepts are equivalent merely because they can be expressed using similar language. D. CAUSAL COMPLETENESS Extract the important causal chains from the Dumb Doc. For each important chain, check whether the current content preserves: cause intermediate condition mechanism effect implication Ask: Has an important intermediate step disappeared? Has a complex causal chain been compressed into a direct claim? Has “how” disappeared while “what” remains? Has an effect been retained while the reason for that effect has been removed? Has a conclusion become unsupported because the reasoning leading to it was omitted? Use this principle: If the Dumb Doc contains: A → B → C → D and the current content effectively presents: A → D check whether B and C were necessary parts of the author's reasoning. E. MECHANISM & PROCESS FIDELITY Identify mechanisms and processes that are important to the Dumb Doc. Ask: What happens? How does it happen? What drives it? What regulates it? What sequence does it follow? Has the mechanism been preserved? Has it been reduced to a vague statement about the outcome? Has a regulatory rule disappeared? Has a process been converted into an unsupported assertion? Do not treat a mechanism as optional simply because the resulting explanation is shorter without it. F. AUTHORIAL INTENT & INTELLECTUAL DIRECTION Ask: Where did the Dumb Doc intend to take the reader intellectually? Does the current content lead toward the same destination? Has the central question changed? Has the scope of the argument changed? Has the philosophical, conceptual, analytical, or metaphysical direction changed? Has the current content introduced an interpretation that was not part of the Dumb Doc? Has an important authorial position been diluted? Do not infer a new authorial intention when the Dumb Doc is ambiguous. Flag ambiguity instead. G. SCOPE & QUALIFICATION Compare the scope of claims in the Dumb Doc with the current content. Check whether: a possibility has become a certainty a qualified statement has become universal a contextual statement has become general a general statement has been unnecessarily restricted a nuanced claim has become absolute H. CONTENT FUNCTION CHECK When content has been removed or compressed, ask: What function did it perform in the Dumb Doc? Was it: a foundation a prerequisite an argument an explanation a mechanism a distinction an example an analogy a transition a qualification an implication a conclusion a preparation for a later concept Do not classify content as unnecessary until its function has been considered. IMPORTANT RULE: Every substantive idea in the Dumb Doc does not need to appear in exactly the same words or in exactly the same location. However, every substantive idea must have a clear fate. If it is absent, determine whether it was legitimately omitted or whether its omission creates a fidelity or completeness problem. ================================================== DUMB DOC FIDELITY STATUS For each important substantive element from the Dumb Doc, classify it as: Preserved The idea and its intended function remain substantially intact. Compressed The idea has been shortened, but its meaning and function remain intact. Implicit The idea is not stated directly, but the current content still communicates it adequately. Weakened The idea remains, but its force, precision, nuance, or explanatory function has been reduced. Altered The idea remains but its meaning, scope, or function has changed. Missing The substantive idea is absent. Distorted The idea has been represented in a way that materially changes or misrepresents its original meaning. ================================================== LENS 6 SCORING RUBRIC Score the following dimensions from 0 to 10: Substantive Content Fidelity — 20% Conceptual Fidelity — 20% Conceptual Distinction — 15% Causal Completeness — 20% Mechanism & Process Fidelity — 15% Authorial Intent & Scope — 10% Scoring guide: 10 — Complete fidelity; no meaningful substantive or conceptual loss. 9 — Very strong fidelity; minor compression without meaningful loss. 8 — Strong fidelity; some minor omission or weakening. 7 — Generally faithful, but several noticeable weaknesses exist. 6 — Noticeable content loss or conceptual weakening. 5 — Moderate fidelity problem; the thesis remains but the conceptual architecture is incomplete. 4 — Significant omission or distortion. 3 — Major conceptual loss; only part of the original intellectual content survives. 2 — Very low fidelity; the original conceptual architecture is largely absent. 1 — Almost no meaningful correspondence with the Dumb Doc. 0 — Fundamentally absent, incompatible, or reversed. Calculate a weighted overall score out of 10. Do not inflate the score merely because the writing is elegant. Do not lower the score merely because the current content uses different wording. Judge preservation of substantive meaning and conceptual function. ================================================== PROBLEM TYPES Classify observations primarily as: Content Problem The content itself needs clarification, development, distinction, qualification, or improvement. Structure / Placement / Sequence Problem The content may be valid and useful, but its position, order, context, grouping, transition, or structural role is causing difficulty. Content–Structure Interaction Problem The content and structure interact in a way that creates a problem, but the solution should preserve the substantive content while improving how it is presented, positioned, connected, or explained. Content Fidelity Problem The current content fails to preserve an important substantive idea, conceptual distinction, causal relationship, mechanism, qualification, or authorial meaning from the Dumb Doc. Conceptual Completeness Problem The current content contains the general direction of the Dumb Doc but lacks an important conceptual dependency, reasoning step, mechanism, distinction, or relationship necessary to make the original argument complete. A finding may involve more than one problem type, but identify the primary type. ================================================== OBSERVATION FORMAT For every meaningful observation, provide: Priority Lens Problem Type Where Intended Understanding Actual Reader Understanding Problem Why It Matters Recommended Direction When Lens 6 is involved, also provide where relevant: Dumb Doc Element Current Content Status Original Function Fidelity Gap Do not use this additional information for unrelated lenses. Use these priority levels: P1 — Must Fix A problem that significantly harms clarity, conceptual understanding, reasoning, reader orientation, reader journey, usefulness, depth, intended experience, or preservation of substantive authorial content. For Lens 6, P1 includes: missing core concepts missing central arguments lost conceptual distinctions missing essential causal links missing essential mechanisms materially altered authorial meaning major changes in scope loss of the intended intellectual direction P2 — Should Fix A noticeable weakness that reduces effectiveness but does not damage the core message. For Lens 6, P2 includes: weakened concepts partial causal compression reduced nuance weakened mechanism explanation minor conceptual distortion meaningful but non-foundational omissions P3 — Nice to Fix A relatively minor issue involving wording, rhythm, small repetition, stylistic polish, minor clarity, or non-essential content compression. Do not inflate minor issues into P1 or P2. ================================================== REVIEW DISCIPLINE Do not search for problems merely to produce a longer review. Do not duplicate the same problem under multiple lenses unless the lenses reveal genuinely different consequences. Prioritize substantive reader impact. Do not call important content unnecessary without first understanding its function. Before recommending removal, consider whether the issue can instead be solved through: relocation reordering better context better explanation better hierarchy stronger transition better introduction better distinction repetition management Do not sacrifice important content to solve a structural problem. Do not impose your own intellectual direction. Do not create new arguments. Do not introduce new substantive content. Do not rewrite the content. If the intended meaning is ambiguous and cannot be established reliably, flag the ambiguity rather than inventing an interpretation. Before recommending a change, consider whether the relevant content may have an important function later in the manuscript. When a Dumb Doc is provided, do not treat the current manuscript as the sole authority for determining whether its own content is complete. Cross-check the manuscript against the Dumb Doc. Do not assume that a concept is preserved merely because a similar word appears in the current content. Check meaning, function, relationship, mechanism, and scope. Do not penalize legitimate restructuring if substantive meaning and conceptual relationships remain intact. Do not demand that every sentence, example, or phrase from the Dumb Doc be retained. The test is substantive fidelity, not textual duplication. ================================================== FINAL DIAGNOSTIC SUMMARY After completing the six-lens review, provide: P1 — Must Fix P2 — Should Fix P3 — Nice to Fix Content Problems Structural / Placement / Sequence Problems Content–Structure Interaction Problems Content Fidelity Problems Conceptual Completeness Problems Intended Understanding Gaps Authorial Content at Risk Dumb Doc Elements That Are Missing Dumb Doc Elements That Are Weakened Dumb Doc Elements That Are Altered or Distorted Causal Relationships at Risk Mechanisms at Risk Conceptual Distinctions at Risk Reader Journey Issues Conceptual Dependency / Sequence Issues Overall Diagnosis ================================================== DUMB DOC FIDELITY SUMMARY When a Dumb Doc is provided, also provide: Content Fidelity Score: X/10 Substantive Content Fidelity: X/10 Conceptual Fidelity: X/10 Conceptual Distinction: X/10 Causal Completeness: X/10 Mechanism & Process Fidelity: X/10 Authorial Intent & Scope: X/10 Then provide: Preserved Compressed Implicit Weakened Altered Missing Distorted Do not force every category to contain an item. Use only categories that actually apply. ================================================== SUMMARY TABLE Provide a final table with: Lens কী identify করেছে Problem Type Priority Intended Understanding Actual Reader Issue কেন গুরুত্বপূর্ণ Recommended Direction For Lens 6, include where relevant: Dumb Doc Element Current Content Status Fidelity Gap ================================================== FINAL RULE DETECT → DIAGNOSE → PRIORITIZE → RECOMMEND Do not: REWRITE → ADD NEW CONTENT → CREATE A NEW STRUCTURE → CHANGE THE AUTHOR'S INTELLECTUAL DIRECTION When a Dumb Doc is provided, additionally: CROSS-CHECK → VERIFY FIDELITY → VERIFY CONCEPTUAL COMPLETENESS → IDENTIFY CONTENT AT RISK → PROTECT THE AUTHOR'S SUBSTANTIVE CONTENT CONTENT TO REVIEW [CONTENT_TO_REVIEW_PLACEHOLDER] DUMB DOC [DUMB_DOC_PLACEHOLDER]

It does not perform a new review. It does not rewrite the manuscript. It does not add new substantive content. It does not create a new argument. It does not change the author's intellectual direction. It does not change the intended reader understanding. It does not silently resolve authorial ambiguity. It does not treat textual similarity as content fidelity. Its sole purpose is: 6-Lens Diagnosis → Editing Actions → Protection Requirements → Verification Conditions → Practical Editing Sequence Final principle: Edit the manuscript to better realize its intended design, not to replace that design with a new one. When a Dumb Doc is provided: Protect the author's substantive content. Protect the author's conceptual architecture. Protect important distinctions. Protect causal relationships. Protect mechanisms. Protect scope and qualification. Restore only what the previous review has established as necessary. Do not invent what the Dumb Doc does not establish.
